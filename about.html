Who we are

One masjid, three towns, one community

Craigavon Masjid (مسجد كريقافن) is run by the Muslim Association of Craigavon and sits at the Legahory Centre, in the middle of Craigavon — a short drive from both Portadown and Lurgan.

It is the only mosque in County Armagh. The nearest alternatives are in Belfast, around twenty-five miles away, and in Newry — so for the Muslims of this area, this building is where the five daily prayers are established, where Jumuʿah gathers the community each Friday, where children learn the Qurʾān, and where families turn at the great moments of life, joyful and sorrowful alike.

The congregation is genuinely multi-ethnic. On a Friday you will hear several languages in the same room, prayed together in one row — doctors and shop workers, students and pensioners, families who have been here for decades and others who arrived last year. What binds them is the prayer, and this place.

The masjid is run by volunteers from the community. If you would like to help, or simply want to know more, get in touch — there is always something to do.

Our work

What the masjid provides

A working masjid is more than a prayer hall. Here is what happens under this roof, week in and week out.

Daily prayers & Jumuʿah

The five daily prayers in congregation, with Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ currently prayed together, and the Jumuʿah khuṭbah and prayer every Friday at 13:00.

Prayer times

Education

Qurʾān and Islamic studies classes for children, and learning for adults too — knowledge passed on across the generations.

Classes

Women’s prayer space

A dedicated prayer space for women, with wudū facilities — the masjid is for the whole community, not half of it.

Facilities

Janāzah support

When a family loses someone, the masjid is there — janāzah facilities, the funeral prayer and support with burial arrangements, handled with dignity.

Funeral support

Ramadan & Eid

Tarāwīḥ prayers, Ramadan meals shared at the masjid, and Eid prayers that fill the hall twice a year — the high points of the calendar.

Seasonal programmes

An open door

Neighbours, school groups and anyone curious about Islam are welcome to visit. Ask your questions — no pressure, no expectations, and usually a cup of tea.

Context

Muslims in Northern Ireland

The Muslim community here is small but growing quickly. At the 2001 census there were around 1,900 Muslims in Northern Ireland; by the 2021 census there were 10,870 — a more than fivefold increase in twenty years.

Most of that community life has historically centred on Belfast, home to the Belfast Islamic Centre — established in 1978 — and the Northern Ireland Muslim Family Association (NIMFA). Craigavon Masjid grew from the same need, closer to home: a place for the Muslims of County Armagh to pray, learn and gather without a fifty-mile round trip.

Today the masjid stands as part of everyday life in this corner of Northern Ireland — one more door on the Legahory Centre, open to its neighbours, and quietly getting on with the work of faith and community.
