Here for every step of life

Daily prayer, marriages, funerals, new beginnings in faith, Ramadan and Eid — the masjid is at your side through all of it. Here is what we offer, and how to reach us.

The daily rhythm

Daily prayers & Jumuʿah

Congregational ṣalāh is the heartbeat of the masjid. We gather for prayer throughout the day, and at present Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ are prayed together in one congregation — a practical arrangement for our long Northern Irish evenings.

On Fridays the community comes together for Jumuʿah: the khuṭbah followed by the prayer, beginning at 13:00. It is the busiest hour of the week at Legahory, so please arrive a little early and park considerately.

If you work nearby in Craigavon, Portadown or Lurgan and would like to join us for any prayer, just come — no appointment needed. First time in a mosque? Our New to Islam page explains what to expect.

Marriage

Nikāḥ — the Islamic marriage

The nikāḥ is a simple, joyful contract: two people commit to one another before Allah, in the presence of witnesses, with family and community around them. We are glad to host nikāḥ ceremonies at the masjid and to guide couples through each step.

How it usually works

  1. Speak to the committee first. Contact us well before you settle on a date, so everything can be arranged properly.
  2. Both parties consent. A nikāḥ is only valid with the free, willing agreement of both bride and groom.
  3. A walī for the bride. The bride is represented by her walī (guardian) — usually her father or a close male relative.
  4. Two witnesses. The contract is made in the presence of two adult Muslim witnesses.
  5. The mahr is agreed. The groom gives the bride a mahr (marriage gift) — agreed between the couple, and hers alone.
  6. The ceremony itself. A short khuṭbah, the offer and acceptance, and duʿāʾ for the new couple — often followed by something sweet to share.

Please note: an Islamic nikāḥ on its own is not a civil marriage in Northern Ireland. To be legally married, couples must also complete civil registration — including giving a marriage notice to the registrar at their local council at least 28 days before the marriage. Contact the committee early and we will help you plan both parts together.

Planning a nikāḥ?

Every couple’s circumstances are a little different, so the first step is always a conversation. We will walk you through the Islamic requirements and the civil ones, in plain terms.

Bereavement

Janāzah — when a loved one passes away

Losing someone is one of the hardest things a family will face. You do not have to face it alone, and you do not need to know what to do — that is what the masjid is for.

In Islam, burial takes place as soon as possible after death, so timing matters. If you are bereaved, or a death is expected, please contact us straight away by phone or WhatsApp and we will begin helping immediately.

How we help

  • Ghusl — the ritual washing of the deceased, carried out with care and dignity.
  • Kafan — shrouding in simple white cloth, as the Sunnah prescribes.
  • Ṣalāt al-janāzah — the funeral prayer, offered by the congregation at the masjid.
  • Burial arrangements — practical guidance for families on what needs to happen and when, and support in liaising with the relevant authorities.

The masjid has janāzah facilities on site, and we will stay alongside your family through the whole process.

New beginnings

Shahādah & support for new Muslims

The shahādah — the declaration of faith — is how a person becomes Muslim, and it can be made here at the masjid with people around you who care. If you are thinking about Islam, or you have recently embraced it, we offer a warm welcome, one-to-one support and honest answers to your questions — at your pace, with no pressure, ever.

The blessed seasons

Ramadan & Eid

The masjid is at its fullest and warmest in Ramadan — long evenings of prayer, shared meals and the two great celebrations of the Islamic year.

Tarāwīḥ

The special night prayers of Ramadan, held in congregation after ʿIshāʾ throughout the month. Everyone is welcome, whether you stay for the whole prayer or part of it.

Ramadan timetable

Ifṭār & Ramadan meals

Community ifṭārs and Ramadan meals bring the whole congregation — and often guests and neighbours — around one table to break the fast together.

An open door

Ask us anything

Not every need fits a neat heading. Perhaps you have a religious question — about ḥalāl food, fasting while unwell, or how to pray at work. Perhaps you are new to the area and unsure where to turn for help. Perhaps you simply want to visit and see the masjid for yourself.

Whatever it is, ask. If we can answer, we will; if it is beyond us, we will do our best to point you to someone who can. And if you are going through a difficult time, reach out — we would rather hear from you early than not at all.
