# Editorial Style Guide

## Status and authority

This guide governs public website copy, search metadata, notices, emails generated by the website, downloadable timetables and administration labels for the Muslim Association of Craigavon.

It is an editorial control, not evidence that any inherited statement is true. A statement may be published only when its source and approval satisfy this guide and, for religious material, [ISLAMIC-CONTENT-VERIFICATION.md](ISLAMIC-CONTENT-VERIFICATION.md).

The official organisation name, public masjid name, logo, contact details and service arrangements are not confirmed at the time of writing. Their status is recorded in [COMMITTEE-CONFIRMATIONS.md](../COMMITTEE-CONFIRMATIONS.md). Editors must not convert an unconfirmed value into a public claim merely because it appeared on the inherited site.

## Editorial principles

Every page should be:

- **Useful:** answer the visitor's practical question before adding background.
- **Specific:** state approved times, requirements, locations, costs, limits and next actions.
- **Honest:** distinguish what the Association provides from what it hopes to provide.
- **Calm:** avoid hype, urgency and emotional promises unless an approved emergency notice requires them.
- **Respectful:** write for worshippers, neighbours, new Muslims, older people, disabled people and people with limited English without patronising them.
- **Maintainable:** give changing information an owner, source, review date and, where appropriate, an expiry date.

## Publication gate

Public content must have all of the following:

1. A named content owner or responsible committee role.
2. A source that is independent of the page being edited.
3. A status of `Confirmed` in the committee register when committee confirmation is required.
4. A review date appropriate to the risk.
5. A plain-English next action.
6. Religious approval where required by the Islamic-content guide.
7. Legal or policy approval where the wording concerns marriage law, safeguarding, privacy, donations or complaints.

Use these working labels in the dashboard and review notes:

| Label | Meaning | Public? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verified fact | Supported by an authoritative source and checked for current accuracy | Yes |
| Committee-confirmation required | Plausible or inherited, but no authorised confirmation is recorded | No |
| Draft | Being written or reviewed | No |
| Approved | Cleared by the required editor/reviewer and within its review period | Yes |
| Expired | Review or end date has passed | No |
| Withdrawn | Removed because it is wrong, superseded or no longer approved | No |

The public website is not an acceptable source for confirming itself. A Facebook post, directory listing or MAWAQIT page may help locate evidence, but it does not replace committee approval for operational information.

## Voice and tone

Write in clear British English. Use `organisation`, `programme` and `centre`; use sentence case for headings and buttons.

Prefer direct, factual language:

| Avoid | Prefer |
| --- | --- |
| We are here for every step of life. | For a nikah enquiry, contact the office before choosing a date. |
| Everyone is welcome. | Visitors may attend by arrangement. Contact us before travelling so a host can be available. |
| We will take it from there. | Call the approved bereavement number. If it is unanswered, follow the alternative route shown here. |
| Classes fill quickly. | Registration is currently open/closed. The committee will confirm availability. |
| Parking is available. | The committee has confirmed [location and restrictions]. Accessible bays: [confirmed detail]. |

Do not guarantee a response, facility, host, place, fee, outcome or opening time. Use an approved response expectation such as “We normally reply within two working days” only when the responsible team has confirmed it can meet that expectation.

### Language to remove

Do not use promotional or template-like phrases such as:

- “at the heart of”;
- “a vibrant community”;
- “embark on your journey”;
- “discover”, “empowering”, “elevate”, “seamless” or “transformative” as vague calls to action;
- “for every step of life”;
- “more than just a mosque”;
- “where faith meets community”;
- “whether you are ... or ...” constructions used as filler;
- “we are proud to” without a specific, evidenced reason;
- “world-class”, “legendary”, “definitive” or “the greatest”;
- “something for everyone”;
- repeated jokes about tea, warmth, family or belonging in place of practical information.

Do not describe the site or platform as unhackable, fully accessible, production-ready, live, automatic or real-time without current evidence defining exactly what was tested.

## Names, identity and geography

- Use **Muslim Association of Craigavon** as the working primary name only while its official form is awaiting confirmation.
- Use **Craigavon Masjid** only as the working public masjid name until the committee confirms it.
- Do not invent an Arabic organisation name or transliteration.
- Do not use “Craigavon Islamic Centre” unless the committee confirms that name.
- Do not claim that the masjid is the only mosque in County Armagh or that it serves every Muslim in an area.
- Do not repeat Craigavon, Portadown and Lurgan throughout headings, body copy and footers. Use place names where they answer a genuine directions, transport, service-area or search question.
- Historical, demographic and geographical claims require a cited source, the date accessed and an owner responsible for future review.

## Plain English and Islamic terms

Use the familiar English term first when it improves understanding:

- Friday prayer (Jumu'ah)
- congregational prayer time rather than `jama'ah` in a navigation label
- funeral support (janazah)
- marriage enquiry (nikah)
- ablution (wudu)

Do not require a visitor to understand academic transliteration. Diacritics may be used in long-form educational content after a familiar English label, but they should not make navigation, buttons, forms or emergency information harder to read.

Use one spelling consistently within a page. The project terminology list is:

| Public label | Optional first-use form | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Allah | Allah | alternating between “God” and “Allah” without editorial reason |
| Qur'an | Qur'an | decorative Arabic or inconsistent `Quran/Koran/Qurʾān` variants |
| Friday prayer | Friday prayer (Jumu'ah) | unexplained `Jumuʿah` in task labels |
| congregational prayer | congregational prayer (`jama'ah`) | unexplained `iqamah/jama'ah` distinction |
| Ramadan | Ramadan | promotional “blessed season” filler |
| Eid | Eid al-Fitr / Eid al-Adha when the distinction matters | an unspecific “Eid event” |
| new Muslim | new Muslim | revert, convert, brother/sister unless that person prefers it |

Religious terminology and spellings must also pass the Islamic-content review.

## Dates, times and changing information

- Use `13:00` for operational prayer and event times unless user research supports an additional 12-hour display.
- Include the timezone where ambiguity is possible: `Europe/London (GMT/BST)`.
- Use `13 July 2026` in prose and machine-readable ISO dates in data.
- State whether a prayer time is a start time, congregational time or Friday-prayer session.
- Never display a calculated or fabricated congregational time when the value is missing.
- Show `Last confirmed [date]` and the responsible source for prayer, access, service and contact information.
- Temporary notices require a start time, expiry time and owner. Cancellation notices should remain visible long enough to prevent confusion and then move to an archive.
- Avoid “today” or “this Friday” in manually maintained copy unless the system derives it safely in the masjid timezone.

## Writing service pages

Every approved service page should answer, in this order:

1. What the service is.
2. Who it is for.
3. What the Association can provide.
4. What it cannot provide.
5. What information the enquirer needs before contacting the Association.
6. Any confirmed fee, eligibility, venue or access information.
7. The next action and expected response route.
8. Urgent or emergency limitations.
9. Relevant official external resources.
10. When and by whom the page was last confirmed.

Do not publish a service merely because a generic mosque site would normally have it. Nikah, janazah, welfare, imam appointments, classes, visits, Ramadan meals, Eid prayer, volunteering, donations and new-Muslim support each require confirmation.

### Sensitive situations

- **Bereavement:** be compassionate but concise. Do not promise immediate availability. Give a monitored route and an approved alternative if unanswered.
- **Marriage:** separate the Association's service, religious requirements and civil-law information. Link to current official guidance and say the website does not give legal advice.
- **Safeguarding:** do not invite disclosures through a general form. Use only the approved safeguarding route.
- **Health, immigration, finance and legal matters:** signpost to qualified official help; do not imply the Association is providing professional advice.
- **Children:** do not request a child's personal data through a public form unless an approved safeguarding and privacy process is active.

## New Muslim and visitor content

- Separate practical mosque-visit information from theological introduction and ongoing new-Muslim support.
- Do not assume why someone is visiting, their beliefs, family situation or level of knowledge.
- Avoid missionary marketing, pressure, promises of belonging and statements about what “makes” somebody Muslim unless they have passed religious review.
- Describe dress, shoes, prayer observation, entrances and facilities as confirmed local arrangements, not universal rules.
- Provide a private approved contact route and state who monitors it and when.
- Do not promise a woman host, one-to-one mentor, class place or continued support unless the responsible team has confirmed capacity.

## News, notices and events

Each notice must include:

- a descriptive title;
- publication date and time;
- owner;
- status and priority;
- effective date/time;
- expiry date/time where temporary;
- last update and a short change note;
- a clear action, if any;
- a source or approver for prayer and emergency changes.

Each event must include the full date, start/end time, timezone, confirmed venue, audience, access information, registration state, cancellation contact and calendar-download data where appropriate. Do not seed fictional events to avoid an empty page.

An urgent notice must say what changed, when it takes effect, who approved it and where to get current help. Do not use an emergency visual treatment for ordinary promotion.

## Links and calls to action

- Use descriptive link text: `Read the monthly prayer timetable`, not `Click here`.
- A button label should describe the result: `Send a visit request`, `Call the bereavement number`, `Download July timetable`.
- Identify file type and size for downloads.
- Identify external services where context matters, especially WhatsApp, Facebook, maps, government guidance and payment providers.
- Do not claim an external integration is official until it is confirmed in the committee register.
- Provide a non-social-media route for essential information.

## Accessible and inclusive writing

- Keep sentences short enough to scan, but do not make serious topics abrupt.
- Use meaningful headings that let a reader understand the page outline out of context.
- Explain abbreviations on first use.
- Do not use colour, position, icons or “above/below” as the only way to identify information.
- Avoid all-capital paragraphs and excessive letter spacing.
- Write useful alternative text for meaningful images; mark decorative images as decorative.
- Do not describe disabled people as “the disabled” or access as “disabled-friendly”. State the confirmed feature: for example, “The entrance has no step and the clear doorway width is [confirmed measurement].”
- Use `lang="ar" dir="rtl"` for Arabic runs and isolate mixed-direction text. See the Islamic-content guide for sacred text.
- Expand symbols and unfamiliar honorifics accessibly; do not rely on a screen reader correctly pronouncing a single Arabic presentation-form glyph.

## Search metadata and structured data

- Titles and descriptions must match the visible page and its confirmed content.
- Do not put unconfirmed phone numbers, addresses, opening times, services, geographic coverage or event data into schema markup.
- Do not keyword-stuff town names.
- Do not hardcode a production domain until the committee confirms it.
- Drafts, preview environments and the administration area must not be indexed.

## Review cadence

| Content | Maximum review interval |
| --- | --- |
| Emergency notice | At every change; expire explicitly |
| Prayer/Jumu'ah/Ramadan/Eid arrangement | Before publication and whenever configuration changes |
| Contact, opening, access and service information | 3 months |
| Classes and recurring programmes | Before each term and at any timetable change |
| Policies and legal signposting | 12 months or when law/policy changes |
| Historical and evergreen background | 12 months |
| External links | 6 months |

The content owner may set a shorter interval. Expired high-risk content should be unpublished automatically rather than silently left online.

## Pre-publication checklist

- [ ] The page answers a real user task.
- [ ] Every factual statement has an appropriate source.
- [ ] Required committee confirmations are recorded as `Confirmed`.
- [ ] Religious material has a completed verification record.
- [ ] Legal, privacy or safeguarding wording has the required specialist approval.
- [ ] No service, facility, contact, time, fee or response is invented or guaranteed.
- [ ] Dates, timezone, owner, last-confirmed date and expiry are present where needed.
- [ ] The next action and urgent limitations are clear.
- [ ] Headings, link text, alternative text, Arabic language/direction and plain English have been checked.
- [ ] Metadata and structured data contain only confirmed values.
- [ ] A second editor has previewed the rendered desktop and mobile page before publication.
